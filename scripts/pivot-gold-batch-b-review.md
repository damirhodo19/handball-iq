## B1. Pivot — Screen the Advanced Defender’s Recovery, Not His First Step

- Family: `pv_51_screen_advanced_recovery_lane`
- Cue: Advanced defender has chased centre back wide and turns to recover through middle
- Structure: 5-1 / 6v6

### EN

**Situation:** You lead 16:15 at 32' against 5:1. The advanced defender chases the centre back toward the right touchline and then turns to recover through the middle. You can arrive in his return lane before he does. Screening his first outward step would have crowded the ball; his recovery is now the useful moment.

**Question:** Where and when do you set the useful screen?

A. **optimal** — Set stationary in his central recovery lane after he turns back
   - Correct — the screen delays recovery without crowding the first attack.
B. **good** — Slip away if you cannot establish both feet before he returns
   - Good late solution; do not chase contact.
C. **risky** — Step into his first outward jump beside the centre back
   - Risky — you bring another body to the pressure side.
D. **poor** — Hold his shirt as he turns back
   - Poor — illegal holding removes the advantage.

**Explanation:** Against 5:1, let the front defender leave and legally delay his recovery. Arrive early, become stationary and keep the central pocket open for the next pass.

**A over B:** A creates the intended screen; B is only the safe correction after the timing is lost.

B. **good** — Otklizaj ako ne možeš postaviti obje noge prije njegova povratka
   - Dobro kasno rješenje; ne lovi kontakt.
C. **risky** — Uđi u njegov prvi izlazak uz srednjeg vanjskog

D. **poor** — Drži mu dres dok se okreće natrag
   - Loše — nedopušteno držanje poništava prednost.

## B2. Pivot — Against 3:3, Short-Roll Behind the First Pressure

- Family: `pv_33_short_roll_after_pressure`
- Cue: Two high defenders trap centre back; central back defender steps to intercept long release
- Structure: 3-3 / 6v6

### EN

**Situation:** You trail 13:14 at 26' against 3:3. Two high defenders trap the centre back. The central back defender steps forward to intercept a long pass toward six metres. A short pocket opens around seven metres directly behind the first pressure.

**Question:** What route gives the trapped passer a safe outlet?

A. **optimal** — Short-roll into the seven-metre pocket and show both hands immediately
   - Correct — the short outlet splits the high trap from the back line.
B. **good** — Continue to six only if the back defender stays deep
   - Good against deep cover; here he has stepped up.
C. **risky** — Run all the way behind the back line and demand a lob
   - Risky — the trapped passer cannot safely throw that distance.
D. **poor** — Join the trap at nine metres for a hand-off
   - Poor — you add traffic to the pressure instead of escaping it.

**Explanation:** The answer to a high 3:3 trap is a short target behind it. Stop in the pocket the passer can reach, receive and then attack the separated back line.

**A over B:** A matches the stepped back defender; B works only if that defender stays deep.

   - Risky — the free space behind the switch disappears.
D. **poor** — Move the screen sideways into the chasing defender
   - Poor — moving contact is an attacking foul.

**Explanation:** A screen in man defence has two reads: hold against chase, slip against switch. The instant your marker leaves, release into the space behind him.

## B20. Pivot — Show the Screen, Then Slip When the Defender Pre-Switches

- Family: `pv_fake_screen_and_slip`
- Cue: Pivot approaches legal screen spot; own defender leaves early to pre-switch onto back before contact
- Structure: 6-0 / 6v6

### EN

**Situation:** You trail 24:25 at 48'. You approach a legal screen spot for the centre back. Before any contact, your defender leaves you and steps early onto the ball carrier to pre-switch. The lane behind him to six metres is open, while completing the screen would contact nobody useful.

**Question:** How do you convert the pre-switch?

A. **optimal** — Abort the screen and slip immediately behind your defender with a visible target
   - Correct — his early switch frees the screener before contact.
B. **good** — Set the screen only if your defender stays and the ball marker chases
   - Good against the original coverage; it has changed.
C. **risky** — Complete the screen at the empty spot and wait
   - Risky — the defence gets the pre-switch for free.
D. **poor** — Chase your defender and hit him from behind
   - Poor — late contact is illegal and misses the open lane.

**Explanation:** A screen is a read, not a destination. If your defender pre-switches before contact, the best screen is no contact: slip into the space he abandoned.

**A over B:** A answers the observed pre-switch; B applies only if the original coverage remains.

### HR

**Situation:** Gubite 24:25 u 48. minuti. Prilaziš legalnom mjestu za blok srednjem vanjskom. Prije ikakvog kontakta tvoj branič te napušta i rano izlazi na igrača s loptom radi preuzimanja. Linija iza njega prema šest metara je otvorena, a dovršavanje bloka ne bi pogodilo korisnog braniča.

**Question:** Kako koristiš rano preuzimanje?

A. **optimal** — Odustani od bloka i odmah otklizaj iza svog braniča s vidljivom metom
   - Točno — njegovo rano preuzimanje oslobađa blokera prije kontakta.
B. **good** — Postavi blok samo ako tvoj branič ostane, a čuvar lopte prati
   - Dobro protiv početnog pokrivanja; ono se promijenilo.
C. **risky** — Dovrši blok na praznom mjestu i čekaj
   - Rizično — obrana dobiva rano preuzimanje bez cijene.
D. **poor** — Lovi svog braniča i udari ga s leđa
   - Loše — kasni kontakt je nedopušten i propušta otvorenu liniju.

**Explanation:** Blok je čitanje, ne odredište. Ako tvoj branič preuzme prije kontakta, najbolji blok je bez kontakta: otklizaj u prostor koji je napustio.

**A over B:** A odgovara viđenom ranom preuzimanju; B vrijedi samo ako početno pokrivanje ostane.
